Debrief on a big screen

I went through every screen at 1920×1080 (your monitor, judging by your screenshots) and at 1440×900 (a laptop), and measured where everything sits. The app is tidy and lines up well on its edges. The problems are about space: a narrow column on a wide screen, a tall top, and side-by-side panels that don't match in height.

Nothing here is built yet. The page maps are drawn to scale; the red dashed line is the bottom of a 1920×1080 screen, so anything under it needs scrolling.

64%of your screen's width is used. The app is a 1240px column, so on 1920 about 330px stays empty on each side.
386pxof every report goes to the top (header, banner, pull strip, tabs) before any results: more than a third of the screen.

Mechanics: the first mechanic on the first screen

Before the first mechanic there are 163px of tiles and a 248px copy of "What went wrong / right" from Latest pull, so the list of mechanics starts at 892px. A one-line scoreboard instead of the tiles, without the copy, brings the list up to about 420px.

Performance: the player bars on the first screen

On top of the bars sit the four kind buttons (80px), two tiles (83px), Damage checks (125px), the chart (230px) and the view buttons. Three changes bring the bars up by about 400px:

  • the kind buttons and the phase chips share one row;
  • Damage checks fold into one line (for example "Damage checks: ✕ 1 failed ▸") that opens on a click;
  • the chart is a little shorter (180px).

Click a player near the bottom of the bars and their breakdown opens at the top of the right column, out of sight. The bars column is 645px tall and the details 279px. Pinning the details panel as you scroll keeps it next to whoever you clicked.

Assignments: fill the tile row

Six tiles sit in a row made for five, so "Healing cooldowns" ends up alone on a second row. Use six columns, or three by two. The same "What went wrong / right" imbalance shows up here, with 4 items next to 1, and the same fix applies.

Small

Summary: two columns instead of one long page

Summary is 3,255px tall, all in one column. That's the report card, the night's chart, what ended pulls, then a Mechanics part and an Assignments part, each with its own report card and phase lists. On a big screen, the report cards and a "jump to" list could sit in a column on the right that stays in view, with the chart and the lists on the left. That roughly halves the length and keeps the grades visible while you read.

Player audit: MVP cards in one row

The MVP cards come in three rows by role. The tank row has one card and empty space for two more. On a wide screen, all six fit in one row (1 tank, 2 healers, 3 DPS), with the role over each card. That saves about 300px and reads like a podium.

Small

Progress: three columns and a way back

The boss cards sit two to a row (613px each), so ten bosses make a 2,277px page. At 1600px and wider, three columns fit, and the page gets about a third shorter. Progress also has no tab bar, so the only way back to the report is the menu. A "‹ Back to the report" link at the top fixes that.

Small

Small polish

  • Content jumps between tabs. Most tabs start their content at 404px, but Summary and Player audit start at 397px, so switching tabs nudges everything. Start all tabs at the same height.
  • Gaps between panels vary from 8px to 23px. Use one gap (14px) between panels, and a bigger one (24px) before a phase heading.
  • The start page sits 8px off the header's left edge (340px vs 332px for the logo).
  • The pull strip's first card is clipped under the ‹ arrow ("…17 …0%"). Keep the arrows outside the cards, or fade the edge.
  • Tooltips on tab names pop up over the page when the mouse rests on a tab. The names explain themselves, so drop them.
  • Mechanics tiles: 11 tiles in rows of 6 leave one empty slot. This goes away with the scoreboard line in the Mechanics proposal.

Suggested order

  1. Quick, for Monday: the small polish list, "Fix first" beside "What went wrong" (Latest pull and Assignments), the pinned Performance details, the Assignments tile row, and Progress's back link and three columns.
  2. Next: the compact banner, the pinned bar with ‹ › pulls, and the Mechanics and Performance reshuffles. These are the ones that move results onto the first screen.
  3. After launch: the wider column on big screens, the two-column Summary and the one-row MVP cards. These are bigger, and worth seeing on your own screen first.
